Nigeria Food Price Forecasting

Exploratory analysis of 20+ years of Nigerian food price data, as the first step toward a full 
forecasting and supply chain optimization system for agricultural markets.

Dataset
World Food Programme (WFP) Nigeria food price monitoring data — 51,000+ price records, 
42 commodities, 14 states, 55 markets, 2002–2023. ([https://www.kaggle.com/datasets](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/awojidemargaret/nigerian-food-prices-2002-2025)

 Key Findings

1. Maize prices swing ~30% every year, on a predictable schedule
Prices bottom out in **February** (post-harvest glut) and peak in **June** (lean season, before 
the next harvest) — the same pattern repeats every year across two decades of data.

![Seasonality](download%20(1).png)

2. Markets 500+ km apart move almost in lockstep
Wholesale maize prices in Kano (Dawanau — a major northern grain hub), Lagos, and Oyo correlate 
at **0.987+** with each other, despite serving very different roles in the supply chain.

![Cross-state price comparison](download%20(2).png)

This suggests Nigeria's maize market is well-integrated — price signals move fast across regions — 
which matters for anyone building forecasting or logistics tools: a shared national model may 
outperform 14 separate state-level ones.
